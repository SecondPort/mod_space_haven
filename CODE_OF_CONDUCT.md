# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Criticise code, never people. Assume the person on the other side is
trying to help and is short on time.

## What is expected

- Talk about the change, not the person who wrote it. "This drops units when the
  container is nested" is useful. "This is sloppy" is not.
- Accept that a review is about the code. Questions on your pull request are the
  process working, not an accusation.
- Give people room to be new. Everyone writes their first Go, their first test,
  their first pull request somewhere.
- Take the point when someone is right, and say so plainly.
- Remember nobody here is paid for this. A reply may take days.

## What is not

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in public or in private.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Discrimination or demeaning remarks about who someone is — nationality,
  gender, sexuality, disability, age, religion, appearance, level of experience.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption: reopening settled arguments, brigading a thread, or
  demanding attention from maintainers.

## Where this applies

Everywhere the project happens: issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, and any space where you are representing the project.

## Reporting

Report anything that breaks these rules to **lucasg.antigravity@gmail.com**.

Reports are read by the maintainer. Say what happened, where, and when — links
help. You will get a reply, and what you send stays private. Reporting something
in good faith will never be held against you, whatever the outcome.

## What happens next

The maintainer decides the response, in proportion to what happened:

1. **A private word** — an explanation of what was wrong and why.
2. **A warning** — with a period of no interaction with the people involved.
3. **A temporary ban** — from all project spaces.
4. **A permanent ban.**

Serious or repeated behaviour skips straight to the later steps.
